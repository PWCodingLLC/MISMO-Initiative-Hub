Work Product Overview

What is the Tri-Party eNote Bailee Agreement?

In paper-based note delivery, "bailee letters" notify parties that a promissory note is being transferred under bailee — subject to the notifying party's continuing interest until funding occurs. With eNotes, the industry has adopted a "Master Bailee Agreement" (MBA) to serve the same function: a tri-party agreement among a lender, the lender's warehouse lender, and the lender's investor, under which the investor acknowledges it holds control and location of the authoritative copy of the eNote in a custodial capacity until it disburses funds to purchase the loan.

This initiative proposes a MISMO-developed, consensus-based industry template MBA — a standardized reference (not legal advice) that clarifies intent and aligns the industry on a core set of terms, so trading partners start from a shared baseline rather than renegotiating foundational concepts for every relationship.

The scale problem is real: a typical lender maintains 5–7 warehouse lines and delivers loans to 12–20 non-GSE investors, requiring negotiation and execution of at least 60 substantively similar MBAs. The friction has been significant enough that some lenders have deprioritized or paused their eNote adoption efforts altogether — making this one of the few remaining barriers to broader market adoption of eNotes.

Why is MISMO working on this?

  1. Reduced outside legal review and negotiation. Financial-services outside counsel rates frequently exceed $1,000/hour, and contract negotiation is one of the largest, most repeatable categories of external legal spend — an estimated $10,000–$25,000 in avoided outside counsel cost per agreement.
  2. Reduced internal legal and onboarding effort. Executing these agreements requires coordination across legal, capital markets, operations, compliance, and technology teams — an estimated $5,000–$15,000 per agreement in avoided internal labor and rework.
  3. Cost has become a barrier to eNote adoption itself. The expense and friction of negotiating these agreements has been significant enough that some lenders have deprioritized or paused their eNote efforts altogether — making MBA cost and complexity one of the few remaining barriers to broader market adoption of eNotes.
