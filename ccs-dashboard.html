Work Product Overview

What is the Common Confidence Score?

The Common Confidence Score is a standardized probability — the estimated likelihood that an AVM value falls within ±10% of a property's market value as of the AVM's effective date. Today, confidence scores differ by provider in both scale and meaning (one might say "92," another "B," another "High" for the same property) — the Common Confidence Score gives every provider one shared scale, expressed as a percentage from 0–100%.

Providers can keep offering their own proprietary scores or Forecast Standard Deviation (FSD) alongside it — the Common Confidence Score is meant to sit next to those, not replace them, while being far easier to interpret than FSD's underlying statistics.

Why is MISMO working on this?

  1. Inconsistent scores make AVMs hard to compare. Three providers can score the same property "92," "B," and "High" — there's no way to line those up against each other or set a consistent risk threshold across vendors.
  2. The accurate alternative is too complex to adopt. Forecast Standard Deviation (FSD) is statistically sound and already used by many providers, but its complexity has limited how widely lenders actually use it.
  3. A 2024 federal rule raised the stakes. The Dodd-Frank AVM quality control rule directs lenders to ensure "a high level of confidence" in AVM estimates — without a shared scale, that's difficult to demonstrate consistently across providers.

Who brought this to MISMO?

Brought by: AVM providers, testers, and lenders, in response to the federal AVM quality control rule

Date started: ~August 2024 — the workgroup met roughly 40 times over the following year before its first release

Why: To create one shared, easy-to-interpret confidence measure across AVM providers, ahead of the Oct. 1, 2025 effective date of federal AVM quality control standards.

Status: ⚑ Published — though the federal rule it responds to is not currently being enforced
